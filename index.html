Doctoral Research

Research

My doctoral research sits at the intersection of cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. I apply data-driven and machine learning methods to understand how software vulnerabilities and weaknesses emerge, evolve, and can be anticipated.

Focus Area

Vulnerability & Weakness Trend Analysis

Studying patterns in publicly reported vulnerabilities and weakness categories over time, with a focus on industrial control and operational technology (ICS/OT) systems, and forecasting how they are likely to develop.

Focus Area

AI & Forecasting for Security

Applying machine learning and time-series forecasting to cybersecurity data to support earlier, better-informed security decisions.

Focus Area

Early-Warning Threat Scoring

Combining forecasts with other signals into a score that highlights which weakness categories deserve attention first, so defenders can prioritize before problems peak.

Focus Area

Language Models for Security Advisories

Exploring how domain-adapted language models can learn from the stream of security advisories over time and help summarize and anticipate emerging issues.

Focus Area

Risk & Threat Assessment

Turning vulnerability analysis into practical risk and threat assessment, a thread that also runs through my applied work at the Miners Cybersecurity Clinic.
